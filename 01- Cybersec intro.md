What is cybersecurity?
Cybersecurity is the practice of safeguarding computers, networks, software applications, critical systems, and data from potential digital threats. Organizations are responsible for securing data to maintain customer trust and meet regulatory compliance. They use cybersecurity measures and tools to protect sensitive data from unauthorized access, and to prevent disruptions in business operations because of unwanted network activity. Organizations implement cybersecurity by streamlining digital defense amongst people, processes, and technologies.
Why is cybersecurity important?
Businesses in various sectors—such as energy, transportation, retail, and manufacturing—use digital systems and high-speed connectivity to provide efficient customer service and run cost-effective business operations. Just like they secure their physical assets, organizations must also secure digital assets and protect their systems from unintended access. An intentional event of breaching and gaining unauthorized access to a computer system, network, or connected facilities is called a cyber attack. A successful cyber attack results in the exposure, theft, deletion, or alteration of confidential data. 
Cybersecurity measures defend against cyber attacks and provide the following benefits.
Prevent or reduce the cost of breaches
Organizations that implement cybersecurity strategies can minimize the consequences of cyber attacks that might impact business reputation, financial standings, business operations, and customer trust. For example, companies activate disaster recovery (DR) plans to contain possible intrusions and minimize disruption to business operations.
Maintain regulatory compliance
Businesses in specific industries and regions must comply with regulatory requirements to protect sensitive data against possible cyber risks. For example, companies that operate in Europe must comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which expects organizations to take appropriate cybersecurity measures to ensure data privacy.
Mitigate evolving cyber threats
Cyber attacks evolve alongside the changes in technologies. Criminals use new tools and devise new strategies for unauthorized system access. Organizations should employ and upgrade their cybersecurity measures to keep up with these new and evolving digital-attack technologies and tools.
What types of attacks does cybersecurity defend against?
Cybersecurity professionals strive to contain and mitigate existing and new threats that infiltrate computer systems in different ways. The following examples describe some common cybersecurity threats.
Malware
Malware stands for malicious software. It includes a range of software programs that are built to allow third parties to gain unauthorized access to sensitive information, or to interrupt the normal workings of a critical infrastructure. Common examples of malware include Trojans, spyware, and viruses.
Ransomware
Ransomware refers to a business model and a wide range of associated technologies that bad actors use to extort money from entities. Whether you’re getting started on AWS or already building cloud infrastructure, you can use various AWS and third-party resources that are dedicated to helping you protect your critical systems and sensitive data against ransomware.
Man-in-the-middle attack
A man-in-the-middle attack involves an outside party that attempts to get unauthorized access through a network during a data exchange. These types of attacks increase the security risks of sensitive information, such as financial data.
Phishing
Phishing is a cyber threat that uses social engineering techniques to trick users into revealing personally identifiable information (PII). An example of phishing is when cyber attackers send emails that users interact with to enter credit-card data on a fake payment webpage. Phishing attacks can also result in users downloading malicious attachments, which then install malware on company devices.
DDoS
A distributed denial of service attack (DDoS) is a coordinated effort to overwhelm a server by sending a high volume of fake requests. DDoS events prevent normal users from connecting to or accessing the targeted server.
Insider threat
An insider threat is a security risk that’s introduced by personnel with bad intentions within an organization. The personnel possess high-level access to the computer systems, and have the potential to destabilize the infrastructure’s security from within the organization itself.
OWASP Top Ten
The OWASP Top 10 is a standard awareness document for developers and web application security. It represents a broad consensus about the most critical security risks to web applications.
To learn about the latest top security threats, see OWASP Top Ten.
How does cybersecurity work?
Organizations implement cybersecurity strategies by engaging cybersecurity specialists. These specialists assess the security risks of existing computing systems, networks, data storage, applications, and other connected devices. Then, the cybersecurity specialists create a comprehensive cybersecurity framework and implement protective measures in the organization.
A successful cybersecurity program involves educating employees on security best practices and using automated cyber defense technologies for existing IT infrastructure. These elements are designed to work together to create multiple layers of protection against potential threats on all data access points.
What are the different types of cybersecurity?
Cybersecurity comprises various strategies that focus on different areas. The different types of cybersecurity can identify risk; protect identities, infrastructure, and data; detect anomalies and events; respond to and analyze root causes; and help with recovery after an event.
A robust cybersecurity approach within an organization addresses the following concerns.
Critical infrastructure cybersecurity
Critical infrastructure cybersecurity refers to cybersecurity protection that focuses on digital systems that are important to society. These critical infrastructure systems include digital systems in energy, communication, and transportation. Organizations in these areas require a systematic approach to cybersecurity because any interruption or data loss can destabilize society itself.
Network security
Network security is cybersecurity protection for computers and devices that are connected to a network. IT teams use network security technologies (such as firewalls and network access control) to regulate user access and manage permissions for specific digital assets.
Cloud security
Cloud security describes the measures that an organization takes to protect data and applications that run in the cloud. Cloud security is important to strengthening customer trust. It can help the organization ensure fault-tolerant operations and comply with data privacy regulations in a scalable environment. A robust cloud security strategy involves shared responsibility between the cloud vendor and the organization.
IoT security
The term Internet of Things (IoT) refers to electronic devices that operate remotely on the internet. For example, a smart alarm that sends periodic updates to your smartphone is considered to be an IoT device. IoT devices introduce an additional layer of security risk because they are constantly connected and might also have hidden software bugs. Thus, an organization must introduce security policies on the network infrastructure to assess and mitigate the potential risks of different IoT devices.
Data security
Data security protects data in transit and at rest with a robust storage system and secure data transfer. Developers use protective measures (such as encryption and isolated backups for operational resilience against possible data breaches. In some cases, developers use AWS Nitro System for storage confidentiality and restricting operator access.
Application security
Application security is a coordinated effort to strengthen an application's protection against unauthorized manipulation during the design, development, and testing stages. Software programmers write secure code to help prevent bugs that can increase security risks.
Endpoint security
Endpoint security addresses security risks that occur when users access an organization's network remotely. Endpoint security protection scans files from individual devices, and is designed to mitigate threats upon detection.
Disaster recovery and business continuity planning
Disaster recovery (DR) and business continuity planning describe contingency plans that allow an organization to respond promptly to cybersecurity incidents, while also allowing the organization to continue operating with little or no disruptions. These plans implement data recovery policies to respond positively to data losses.
End-user education
People within an organization play a crucial role in helping to ensure the success of cybersecurity strategies. Education is key so that employees are trained to follow good security best practices, such as deleting suspicious emails and not plugging in unknown USB devices.
What are modern cybersecurity technologies?
The following modern cybersecurity principles and technologies help organizations secure their data.
Zero trust
Zero trust is a cybersecurity principle that assumes that no applications or users are trusted by default, even if they are hosted within the organization. Instead, the zero-trust model assumes that access control is based on the principle of least privilege, which requires both strict authentication from the relevant authorities and continuous application monitoring. AWS uses zero-trust principles to authenticate and validate each individual API request.
Behavioral analytics
Behavioral analytics monitor data transmission from devices and networks to detect suspicious activities and abnormal patterns. For example, an IT security team is alerted when a sudden spike in data transmission occurs, or when the system detects many downloads of suspicious files to specific devices.
Intrusion detection systems
Organizations use intrusion detection systems to identify and quickly respond to a cyber attack. Modern security solutions use machine learning and data analytics to uncover dormant threats in the organization's computing infrastructure. The intrusion defense mechanism can also pick up a data trail if an incident occurs. The data trail can then help the security team discover the source of the incident.
Cloud encryption
Cloud encryption scrambles data before it’s stored in cloud databases, which helps prevent unauthorized parties from abusing the data in possible breaches. Organizations can use AWS Key Management Service (AWS KMS) to control data encryption in AWS workloads.
